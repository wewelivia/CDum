Subject: Top of mind: hike risk into CPI, the oil grind, and a two-sided AI tape

Team,

Are central banks really about to hike into a slowing world, and does the yield backup matter? Friday's US payrolls were a blow-out at +162K against roughly 55K expected, with unemployment holding at 4.1% and wage growth benign. That keeps a 16 September Fed hike live (market odds moved to about 60%), even after dovish signals from Waller and Williams. The ECB is set to lift its deposit rate a quarter point to 2.50% on Thursday, likely the last move of its short cycle, and the BOJ looks poised to go again this month, with JGB 10y yields above 3% for the first time since 1996. The through-line is a global backup in long yields driven by fiscal supply and AI-related issuance, not by growth strength. Our view: the hike itself is not the risk, sticky inflation is. We would treat Friday's US CPI as the real swing factor and stay up in quality and light on duration.

Is the oil and Iran story about to break something? Kinetic exchanges in the Strait of Hormuz resumed and Brent pushed to around $96, up close to 9% on the week. The tell is not crude, it is diesel, which hit a record $5.85 a gallon, up roughly 60% on the year, feeding straight into freight and food costs. Oil is still transiting Hormuz under US naval cover, so this is a slow tax on growth rather than a 2022-style spike. Our view: we would fade panic on any single headline but keep energy and defensives as a hedge, and watch diesel cracks rather than the Brent print for the real signal.

With AI capex booming, why is the tape two-sided? Broadcom and Dell confirmed the demand is real (Broadcom now guides AI chip revenue to around $230bn by FY28, Dell's AI server backlog hit $95bn). Yet model competition is brutal, with OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra out and Google and Meta close behind, and the Anthropic S-1 now slipped to late September. Capex is outrunning cash flow and leaning on debt, which is part of what is lifting yields. Our view: this is starting to look less like a monopoly franchise and more like a capital-intensive utility. We stay long the pick-and-shovel infrastructure names and more selective on the model layer.

One to watch: European politics and fiscal risk, with the ECB decision Thursday, France's 2027 budget fight and UK gilt jitters under PM Burnham all live, plus Sunday's Saxony-Anhalt vote where the AfD leads.

Best, Ju

Risk case, a hot CPI plus a September hike and a fresh oil leg tightens financial conditions fast, and the crowded AI complex is where the pain would concentrate. We stay invested, up in quality, and hedged with energy.
