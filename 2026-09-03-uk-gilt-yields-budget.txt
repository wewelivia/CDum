The gilt market has become the most expensive in the G7. Thirty-year yields reached 5.85%, a level last seen in 1998, while the 10-year traded just above 5.2%, the highest since August 2007. This matters for reasons beyond bond investors. Higher yields raise the cost of servicing government debt, which shrinks the room available for spending. Deutsche Bank estimates the Treasury's headroom against its own fiscal rule would fall from around £26 billion at the spring forecast to £13.8 billion at current yields, ahead of the first Budget from Andy Burnham and Chancellor John Healey on 28 October.
