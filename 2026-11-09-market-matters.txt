Market Matters — draft for Monday 9 November 2026

What’s moving markets?
Blue wave, red screens

Last week we flagged the US midterm elections as the event to watch. Democrats won both the House and the Senate, the first time both chambers have flipped in a midterm since 2006 (CS Monitor, 4 Nov).

Markets cheered first and worried later. The Dow Jones rose 3% on Wednesday, its best day after a midterm since 1982 (Yahoo Finance, 5 Nov). On Thursday the S&P 500 fell 4.36% and the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52% (Yahoo Finance, 5 Nov). For both indices it was the worst day since April 2025 (StockTitan, 5 Nov). Shares recovered part of the loss on Friday. The S&P 500 closed at 7,376.86, down 2.7% for the week, its worst week since April 2025 (AP via AsiaOne, 7 Nov).

The worry is AI infrastructure. Investors fear a Democratic Congress could tighten the rules on data centres, the buildings that house the computers powering AI. Local voters backed limits in 40 of the 41 ballot measures on data centres (State Capitol Lobbyist, citing Ballotpedia, 5 Nov).

Oil sheds some of its war premium

Last week we noted oil above $110 a barrel and growing concern about further military action. Brent crude settled at $98.32 on Friday (eOption, 6 Nov). That left it down around 13% for the week on comparable contracts, its steepest weekly fall since the start of the pandemic (eOption, 6 Nov). Traders hoped the election result would hasten an end to the war with Iran.

Hopes and risks rose together. Qatar has put a two-day confidence-building plan to both sides, ending with Iran reopening the Strait of Hormuz (AP via Business Standard, 7 Nov). On Friday night, though, President Trump described a “minor” US strike on Iranian air defences, while insisting that talks were making progress (Euronews, 7 Nov).

The Bank of England moves first

As we flagged last week, the Bank of England (BoE) raised interest rates by 0.25 percentage points to 4%. It was the BoE’s first increase since August 2023 (Bank of England, 5 Nov). The BoE expects inflation to reach around 5% by the middle of next year. Governor Andrew Bailey said that unless the Middle East conflict is resolved, the rise did not seem likely “to be the last increase” (AJ Bell, 5 Nov).

What does this mean for investors?
One industry, one index

On Thursday the PHLX Semiconductor index, which tracks major chipmakers, fell 12.25% (Yahoo Finance, 5 Nov). It was the index’s worst day since March 2020 and left it about 38% below its June record (Yahoo Finance, 5 Nov).

Last week our investment team said AI will increasingly influence the wider economy, so investing need not be a choice between AI companies and everything else. Thursday showed the other side of that link. The same small group of stocks that lifted the indices can also drag them down.

A jobs surprise keeps the Fed in play

The other item on last week’s watch list brought a surprise. The US economy added 165,000 jobs in October and unemployment fell to 4.1% (BLS, 6 Nov). Economists polled by Reuters had expected 65,000 jobs (Reuters, 6 Nov). The figures for August and September were also revised up by a combined 84,000 jobs (BLS, 6 Nov).

According to CME FedWatch, markets now put the chance of a US Federal Reserve (Fed) rate rise in December back near 80%, up from about 67% on Thursday (Reuters, 6 Nov). Government bonds still did their job over the week. The 10-year US yield ended Friday at 4.858%, around 0.07 percentage points lower than a week earlier (eOption, 6 Nov). Bond yields fall when prices rise, so bondholders gained while shares fell.

What are we looking at this week?
US inflation

October’s US consumer price index is due on Tuesday 10 November (BLS). In September prices were up 3.7% on a year earlier. Core inflation, which strips out volatile food and energy prices, was 2.6% (BLS, 14 Oct). This is the last scheduled consumer price report before the Fed meets on 8 and 9 December (TokenPost, 1 Nov).

UK growth

The first estimate of UK growth for July to September arrives on Thursday 12 November. In the three months to August the economy grew 0.7% (ONS, 15 Oct).

Diplomacy under strain

Oil markets reopen on Sunday evening, their first session since Friday night’s strike. Iran says it will not negotiate on its nuclear programme until the US meets its core conditions, which include ending the blockade of its ports and lifting sanctions (AP via Business Standard, 7 Nov).
