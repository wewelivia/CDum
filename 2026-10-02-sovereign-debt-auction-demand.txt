It is important to realise that despite the move higher in bond yields, government debt remains in high demand. When the US and UK sold new paper over the past fortnight, bids typically ran at more than twice the amount on offer. Even the five-year US Treasury auction, the ugliest of them, was covered 2.2 times. What it did not attract was the price the market expected: it cleared 3.1 basis points above where the bond had been trading, the second-widest gap on record, leaving primary dealers with 15.8% of the issue. Germany came closest to a genuine shortfall, allotting €4.2bn of a planned €5.5bn ten-year sale, though its debt agency retains a slice of every auction by design. In other words, it is not that investors do not want sovereign debt, just that they want a bit less of it and to be paid a bit more to hold it.
